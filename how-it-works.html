How the Hub gets a student's info

In the demo, John's data comes from a mock API that runs in your browser. This page shows what would sit behind it at Illinois Tech: where each piece of a student's info comes from, what the Hub keeps, and what it never touches.

From sign-in to matches

Every student starts the same way: sign in, say what the Hub may read, and get a profile that only holds what matching needs. Listings come in from the other side: Handshake postings, kept current by the tracking agent.

  • Student
  • Elevate Hub (what we would build)
  • Illinois Tech systems
  • Stored data
  1. Sign in

    The student picks "Sign in with Illinois Tech Portal". The Hub hands sign-in to Okta (Access Illinois Tech), where they use their Portal password and Okta Verify. The Hub never sees the password.

  2. Learn who they are

    Okta sends back a signed SAML assertion with the student's CWID, name and Illinois Tech email. That is enough to greet them, not enough to match them.

  3. Ask before reading anything else

    The Hub shows what it wants to read (class year, major, work status) and why. If the student says no, they pick those three things themselves and still get matches.

  4. Read the records

    With the student's OK, the Elevate API reads each system with a read-only service account and logs every read.

    • Banner (Registrar): class standing, credit hours, major, minor, college, expected graduation, citizenship category.
    • Handshake (Career Services): resume on file, career appointments, assigned Elevate coach.
    • Elevate program records: experiences completed and digital badges.
  5. International students only

    If Banner shows F-1 or J-1 status, the Hub asks the Office of Global Services whether CPT or OPT is on file and for which dates. Nothing else from immigration records.

  6. Keep only what matching needs

    The profile builder turns those records into a handful of fields: year, major group, work status and interests. Raw records are not copied into the Hub.

  7. Save it with a paper trail

    The Elevate database stores the profile, what the student agreed to and when, and an audit log of every read. Banner is re-checked each night, so a change of major updates matches the next morning.

  8. Match and act

    The matching service scores every program in the programs table against the profile. The scores drive the program feed, the My week to-do list, deadline alerts and calendar files.

Where the listings come from

For now, every internship and job listing comes from Handshake, through Career Services. Employers already post there for Illinois Tech students. Research Match projects come straight from faculty.

  • People
  • Elevate Hub (what we would build)
  • Illinois Tech systems
  • Stored data
  • Handshake is the only source of listings for now. Other sources, like company careers pages or national lab programs, could be added later through the same agent.
  • Most listings publish on their own. Career Services already vets employers in Handshake, so a person only sees what the agent flags: it isn't sure who can apply, the posting limits citizenship or work status, a deadline or eligibility rule changed after it went live, or a student reported a problem.
  • Someone on the Elevate team also spot-checks a few random listings each week.
  • The Hub reads postings through access Career Services arranges with Handshake, not by scraping it. Every listing keeps its Handshake link and a "last checked" date.
  • Faculty projects go live as soon as they're posted. Elevate can still hide one.

What the Hub never collects

  • GPA and grades
  • Social Security number
  • Date of birth and home address
  • Visa, passport or I-20 documents
  • Disability, health or financial aid records

FERPA in one paragraph

Illinois Tech's FERPA page lists what counts as directory information, such as a student's name and dates of attendance. Treat everything past that, like class year, major and citizenship, as part of the education record: the Hub reads it only after the student allows it, under a data-sharing agreement with the Registrar, and logs every read.

Keeping programs current

The "Tracking agent" box in both charts. It does the boring part of watching Handshake for new and changed postings, and sends only the listings it isn't sure about to a person.

  1. Pull

    New and changed Handshake postings for Illinois Tech students.

    Agent
  2. Re-check

    Daily, and hourly in the two weeks before a deadline.

    Agent
  3. Draft

    Tag who can apply and write the next steps. An AI model drafts this.

    Agent
  4. Compare

    New posting, changed deadline, closed, or no change.

    Agent
  5. Flag the unsure ones

    Unclear eligibility, citizenship limits, late changes and student reports go to a person.

    Elevate team
  6. Publish and notify

    Clear listings go live on their own. Students who saved or match one hear about it.

Database tables

One PostgreSQL database for the Hub. Banner, Handshake and Global Services stay the systems of record; the Hub keeps only what it needs to match and remind.

  • studentsCWID, name, email, last Banner sync
  • consentsWhat the student allowed, when, and on which screen
  • student_profilesYear, major group, work status, interests
  • programsTitle, organization, deadline, location, pay, steps, Handshake link
  • eligibility_rulesYears, majors and citizenship each program accepts
  • saved_programsWhich student saved which program
  • checklist_progressWhich steps a student has finished
  • applicationsProgram or project, stage, status
  • appointmentsCoach chats and research interviews
  • todosTasks a coach assigns
  • notificationsUpdates feed and email log
  • research_projectsFaculty projects in Research Match
  • research_applicationsStudent applications to those projects
  • agent_runsWhat the tracking agent pulled from Handshake and changed
  • listing_flagsListings the agent sent to a person, and why
  • audit_logEvery read of a student's records, and by whom

What we'd need from Illinois Tech

None of this is technically hard. Each step needs an office to say yes.

  • Office of Technology ServicesRegister the Hub as an app in Okta (Access Illinois Tech) so students can sign in with the Portal.
  • RegistrarA data-sharing agreement and read-only access to the Banner fields above, after a FERPA review.
  • Office of Global ServicesA yes/no CPT and OPT status for international students, or agreement that the Hub only links out to the ISSS Portal.
  • Career Services and ElevateHandshake access for job postings, appointments and resume status, plus Elevate coach assignments and badge records.
  • FacultyA few departments willing to post projects in Research Match the week they start them.
  • Security and hostingA security review, hosting on Illinois Tech servers, and a plain-language privacy notice students read before they allow access.

Suggested stack

  • Web app: HTML and JavaScript, like the demo
  • API: Node.js or Python on Illinois Tech servers
  • Database: PostgreSQL
  • Sign-in: SAML through Okta
  • Jobs: a scheduler for nightly Banner syncs and the tracking agent
